Subsidy processing with budget reservations

Subsidy requests keep being submitted over the same day-based timeline. A request starts as submitted, then gets a random-looking processing duration between 1 and 3 days before it is accepted or rejected. For 1 out of every 4 requests, the caseworker needs more information after 1 to 2 days of initial processing. That subprocess takes 2 to 3 days plus 1 extra day to resolve, and is followed by 1 to 2 more days of processing before a decision is made. From submission until a rejection, the full amount is always counted as reserved — just like a hotel reservation hold. Payout follows strict first-come-first-served order, but for the 1-in-4 requests that needed a "more information" subprocess, their place in that queue is based on the day the extra information was received, not their original submission day — the reservation itself still starts at the real submission day and runs the whole time. An accepted request cannot be paid or marked unpaid until everyone ahead of it in that order has itself been resolved, even if its own decision already happened. Until then it sits as accepted with payout pending. Once the budget runs out for one request, it stays locked for everyone after it in the queue too — a later, smaller request can never back-fill into space an earlier request couldn't claim. Sliding timeline window

The top row shows subsidy decision days. Each subsidy row shows the submitted phase, processing reserve, and the decision dot — green for accepted (paid, or payout still pending an earlier request), green with a red error mark for accepted but unpaid, and red for rejected. When more than one decision lands on the same day, the dots fan out left to right in the same order the matching rows appear below. For 1 in 4 requests, a pink "more info needed" subrow appears directly below the main row after 1–2 days of processing, spanning 2–3 days plus 1 extra day. Processing then resumes on the main row for 1–2 more days before a decision can be made. Bars only fill in up to the furthest day the simulation has reached — nothing is shown ahead of that, so each row gradually builds up as the simulation plays forward. Dragging the day slider backward while paused lets you look at an earlier moment without hiding anything already revealed; only moving forward past that point reveals more. When a request is accepted but loses out on payout to the very next request ahead of it in the queue, a tight amber ring pops up and blinks around its row, then stays in place as a permanent marker. Subsidy-budget logic in this version

1. Submitted

Every new subsidy request enters the timeline on its submission day. The first day is shown as the submitted state.

2. Processing reserve

From submission until a decision, the full requested amount is always counted as reserved, reducing what shows as available for other requests — regardless of whether the budget can actually cover it.

3. Needs more info (1 in 4)

Every 4th request processes for 1–2 days before triggering a subprocess that requests more information, adding 2 to 3 days plus 1 extra day. Processing then resumes for 1–2 more days before a decision, shown as a pink hatched segment on the timeline. The budget stays reserved throughout — but for payout queue order, this request is treated as if newly submitted the moment the extra information arrives.

4. Payout pending

An accepted request can't be paid or marked unpaid until every earlier-submitted request has itself resolved — even if its own decision already happened. Until then it shows as accepted with payout pending.

5. Accepted & paid

Once a request's turn comes up in submission order, it's paid out if the budget still has the funds — whoever submitted earliest is always checked first, regardless of whose decision lands first.

6. Accepted, unpaid

If, when its turn comes up, the budget has already run out, it stays accepted but unpaid — shown as a green decision dot with a red error mark. Once locked, the budget stays locked for every later-submitted request too, even smaller ones that would otherwise fit.

7. Rejected release

Requests that fail eligibility review are rejected and release their reserve back to the available budget.
